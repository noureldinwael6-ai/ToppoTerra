# Scientific methods

## What the record represents

The series covers monthly statewide temperature and total precipitation for Arizona and California. Treat both as NOAA NCEI Climate at a Glance statewide aggregates. Weather-station records supplied separately do not stand in for either state. The source exports and 1981–2010 reference construction are described in [Data provenance](DATA_PROVENANCE.md).

Calendar months stay in local reporting dates without a timezone conversion. Monthly anomalies subtract each state's corresponding calendar-month mean from 1981–2010. Each retained annual point requires 12 distinct valid months. Annual temperature is a day-weighted mean in °C. Annual precipitation sums monthly totals in mm. Annual anomalies apply the same aggregations to the monthly departures. Missing dates and values are never interpolated. Side-by-side charts describe each state independently. A difference between two significance classifications is not a test of a difference between state trends.

## Conventional trend

- Ordinary least squares regresses annual values on their numeric calendar years with an intercept. The annual coefficient is multiplied by 10 for a rate per decade. Modeled change is the selected fit's annual slope times the number of elapsed years between the first and last complete years. The raw endpoint difference is descriptive.
- Newey–West heteroskedasticity- and autocorrelation-consistent (HAC) covariance uses Bartlett weights, bandwidth floor(4(n/100)^(2/9)), and the finite-sample scale n/(n−2). Pairs are matched by actual calendar-year separation. A Student t reference with n−2 degrees of freedom gives an approximate two-sided p-value and pointwise 95% intervals for the OLS slope and fitted mean. The fitted line may span missing years without imputing observations. Confidence shading represents uncertainty in the fitted mean rather than a prediction interval. Lag-1 residual correlation and p-values with bandwidths one and five years document sensitivity to short-range serial dependence.
- Theil–Sen takes the median of every distinct-year pair slope. Its plotted intercept is the median of the centered intercepts. OLS HAC diagnostics remain OLS diagnostics when the displayed line is Theil–Sen. Mann–Kendall uses a tie-corrected variance, a continuity-corrected normal approximation for p at n ≥ 10, and Kendall tau-b. Its usual independence assumption makes its p-value descriptive in these serial records. Inferential decisions use OLS HAC alone.
- Inference needs at least 20 complete annual years and 80% annual coverage between complete endpoints. A positive residual variance is also required. At p < 0.05 the trend is classified as significant. From p ≥ 0.05 to p < 0.20, agreement between OLS and Theil–Sen on direction produces the exploratory “Possible/emerging change” label. Remaining fits have no supported directional trend. All-flat series report zero directional change. Slope estimates may be available on selections with insufficient inference support. These are project thresholds and per-selection tests. Repeated state, period, method and variable exploration is not multiplicity-adjusted.
- Annual aggregation reduces seasonal confounding. Approximate HAC inference may remain sensitive to high persistence, nonlinear trends, choice of interval, reporting coverage, and structural changes. Significance does not identify cause or establish future conditions.

## Public Explorer figures

- The historical chart displays raw or anomaly values and, for the annual view, a selected OLS or Theil–Sen fit. OLS uncertainty shading describes the fitted mean. Dragging a horizontal region updates the actual selected calendar years and recomputes the statistics. Filters and the latest-decade button expose the same period selection without dragging.
- Comparison uses the same NOAA product, the same selected variable, and common calendar labels. The paired rate fits annual Arizona minus California values on years valid for both states. Newey–West HAC uses residuals of the paired difference, preserving contemporaneous covariance. At 1981–2025 the raw paired temperature rate is +0.02868 °C/decade (95% CI −0.04232 to +0.09968, p=0.420, 45 matched years). The paired precipitation rate is −1.39 mm/decade (95% CI −44.27 to +41.49, p=0.948, 45 matched years). Neither estimate gives evidence of a difference in slopes under these assumptions.
- Calendar-month means pool only finite observations of that month in the selected period. Year × month heatmaps share a common scale when comparing states. Anomaly color is symmetric around zero. Empty cells explicitly identify missing dates.
- Distribution boxes split the calendar range after the floor of the midpoint year. Each half uses complete annual values, requires at least four years and reports linearly interpolated quartiles. The ends of the whiskers are the observed minimum and maximum. The visual compares spread and level descriptively.
- Monthly coverage counts each valid date once against a 12-month calendar. The wettest-month plot shows the annual maximum of raw statewide monthly precipitation totals only in complete years. It describes extremes of statewide monthly totals. Data for daily events or finer-scale geographic coverage are separate requirements.

## Experimental Topo-Signal

Topo-Signal adapts delayed coordinates, multimodal embedding and persistent features from the team's research. The preprint considers sensor signals and physical metrics for fluid media. For climate observations we use ordinary Euclidean distance and leave sensor metrics, adaptive delay, topology-based repair, and physical state inference outside this demonstration. The team's earlier TypeScript engine confirmed the practical pattern of exact bounded Vietoris–Rips reduction over GF(2). This climate implementation uses the same mathematical filtration with a smaller and independent implementation.

1. Use statewide monthly anomalies. Standardize each channel using its own 1981–2010 anomaly mean and sample standard deviation, fixed across windows and selected periods. Each channel needs at least 120 finite baseline months and positive baseline variance. Within a state the joint analysis aligns temperature and precipitation by the exact calendar month before taking delayed coordinates.
2. Use complete windows, with the first start anchored to January. The default interface uses 48 months and a 12-month stride. The controls also support 24, 60, 96 and 120 months, and strides of 6 or 24 months. One missing calendar month in any selected channel removes its window. The default delay is three months. One and six months provide sensitivity views. A single channel uses [z(t), z(t−τ), z(t−2τ)]. Joint windows concatenate that vector for temperature and precipitation, with each channel in standardized units. The camera in joint mode projects the primary variable's three coordinates while persistent homology uses all six. Take up to 30 deterministic, evenly spaced anchor months per window after allowing for the two preceding lags. Selecting an anchor shows its actual monthly rows, their baseline departures, and their standardized coordinates.
3. Build the complete Euclidean Vietoris–Rips filtration of the sampled points through triangles. The filtration value of an edge is its length. A triangle enters when its longest edge enters. H0 uses union–find for the merges, with one essential interval. H1 births are edges closing cycles in the forest. Reduce triangle boundaries over GF(2), projected onto nonforest edges, to pair cycle births and deaths. This projection is a change of cycle basis: every nonforest edge adds one unique fundamental cycle, so each cycle is uniquely specified by its nonforest edges. Only positive-lifetime intervals are plotted. No H2 result is inferred from the two-skeleton. The module caches bounded diagrams by the exact ordered point coordinates.
4. A linked persistence diagram and barcode show positive H0/H1 intervals, with a separate marker for the infinite H0 interval. The interactive filtration slider steps among genuine pairwise distances and plots edges at or below ε, counts all clique triangles, and reads active components and loops from half-open persistence intervals. The geometry can show birth/death scales without attributing a specific climatic cause to a feature.
5. The fixed-reference score uses exact bottleneck distance between finite H1 diagrams of the current window and the earliest complete window of the same width inside the 1981–2010 reference. The adjacent score separately compares immediately neighboring valid windows. A feature may match the diagonal at half its lifetime. Zero means equality of finite H1 diagrams in this representation. The full study anomaly series shows the selected structural window, while the point-source chart shows the original values in that window. The fixed-reference view is retrospective because its anomaly scaling uses the full baseline. The benchmark uses separately frozen train-only transforms and calibration for historical replay.
6. Treat peaks as candidates to inspect. Windows share four years and neighboring values are dependent. Each plotted year names the end of a window. Changing variance, residual seasonality, shifts in persistence, outliers, and sampling can change the geometry. The exploratory distances have no associated significance claim. Five-year windows and fixed lags are design choices for this proof of concept.
7. The exploratory lag plot calculates Pearson correlation between temperature anomaly at t and precipitation anomaly at t+k, for k from −6 to +6 months. It includes only finite date-matched pairs inside the selected state and period. Positive k means precipitation comes later. The plot displays sample counts and uses no independence assumption or causal interpretation.

## Reproducible example

Over 1981–2025, California's full-year precipitation rate is −22.51 mm per decade (95% OLS HAC interval −70.25 to +25.23, p = 0.347). The largest adjacent finite-H1 distance using a five-year window and the three-dimensional, three-month-lag embedding is 0.194, comparing 2008–2012 with 2007–2011. The 2008–2012 window's fixed-reference distance is 0.199. The largest adjacent distance ends in 1995 at a one-month lag and 2014 at a six-month lag. These exploratory rankings change with the embedding parameters.
